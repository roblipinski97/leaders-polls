      Poland since 1997

      Every Sejm voting-intention poll on English Wikipedia across seven parliamentary terms, joined into continuous lines. Party lineages follow renames, mergers and joint lists (PO to KO, SLD to Lewica, KNP to Konfederacja). The band above the chart shows which camp held the prime minister's office.

      How this is made

      Data

      All numbers come from the opinion-polling articles on English Wikipedia, e.g. Opinion polling for the next Polish parliamentary election. A Python pipeline downloads each page through the MediaWiki API. It then finds the national voting-intention tables, skipping seat projections, regional polls, hypothetical scenarios and approval ratings, and reads every row. Each chart links to the exact page revision used. Wikipedia text and tables are available under CC BY-SA 4.0.

      Incumbents

      The highlighted party is the party of the head of government (prime minister, chancellor, taoiseach) on each date. When the office changed hands within a term, the thick line switches party at the handover; examples are Romania 2025 and France 2024. Caretaker and non-party prime ministers draw no thick line. Where the prime minister is formally non-party, the governing camp is used (Serbia's SNS-led list, Moldova's PAS, Romania's PSD since October 2026). Belgium has no national polls, Switzerland has a collegial executive and Cyprus a presidential one, so they are left out.

      Trend lines

      Lines are a local linear regression (a LOESS-type smoother) through the individual polls. Each poll is weighted by the square root of its sample size and by a Gaussian kernel in time. The bandwidth widens where polls are sparse: about three weeks in Germany, several months in Luxembourg. The election result counts as a very heavy poll, so each line starts at the actual result. Where a pollster reports undecided voters inside the 100%, shares are rescaled to decided voters. Polled joint lists (for example Third Way in Poland) are joined to their continuing party.

      Colours

      Each party keeps the colour Wikipedia uses for it, because readers already know those colours. A party can share a colour family with another, like Poland's two navy-blue parties or the many Nordic reds. Then the smaller party is nudged in lightness or hue until the pair is clearly distinguishable in a perceptual colour space (OKLab). Colours that are too pale for a white or dark background are darkened or lifted. Colour is never the only cue: the governing party is drawn thicker, line ends carry labels, and every chart has a legend, a tooltip and a table view.

      Caveats

      Wikipedia tables are maintained by volunteers. Pollsters differ in method and in whether they report undecided voters, and some rows carry errors. The tracker reports the trend, not a forecast. Election dates marked "latest" are legal deadlines, and an early election can be called at any time.
